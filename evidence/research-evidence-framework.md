# Research Evidence & Verification Framework

**MRMProLeads — Evidence-Aware B2B ABM Research**

Good sales intelligence is only useful when the reasoning behind it can be understood and trusted.

MRMProLeads therefore treats evidence as a core part of account research — not as an optional reference field added at the end.

The objective is simple:

**Research → Evidence → Confidence → Sales-Useful Decision**

---

## Why Evidence Matters

A contact database can tell a sales team:

> “Here is a person you can contact.”

Account research should be able to answer:

> “Why this account, why this person, and what supports that conclusion?”

This difference matters when sales teams are prioritizing limited outreach time.

---

# The Evidence Model

```text
Research Finding
      ↓
Source
      ↓
Source Quality
      ↓
Fact / Inference Classification
      ↓
Confidence
      ↓
Sales Use
```

Every important finding should have enough context for another person to understand how the conclusion was reached.

---

# 1. Source Hierarchy

Not every source carries the same evidentiary value.

A practical source hierarchy is:

### Tier 1 — Official Sources

Examples:

* Company website
* Official company announcements
* Regulatory filings
* Government records
* Official public documents

Best used for confirming core company information, leadership, business activities, and official announcements.

### Tier 2 — Public Institutional Sources

Examples:

* Government databases
* Regulatory records
* Municipal records
* Industry organizations
* Public procurement records

Useful when the information is independently documented outside the company website.

### Tier 3 — Professional Sources

Examples:

* LinkedIn
* Professional profiles
* Industry publications
* Conference profiles

Useful for professional context, role history, and organizational signals.

### Tier 4 — Third-Party Data Sources

Examples:

* Contact databases
* Business directories
* Data providers
* Aggregated company databases

Useful for research support, but important information should be independently checked where appropriate.

### Tier 5 — Inference

An inference is an interpretation derived from available evidence.

It should never be presented as a confirmed fact.

---

# 2. Fact vs Inference

One of the most important research distinctions is:

**What do we know?**

versus

**What do we think the evidence may indicate?**

### Confirmed Fact

Information directly supported by an appropriate source.

Example:

> The company publicly describes a specific service or business activity.

### ABM Hypothesis

A reasonable interpretation of observed information.

Example:

> The activity may indicate relevance to a particular business problem.

### Unverified

Information for which sufficient evidence has not yet been established.

### Discrepancy

Two sources provide conflicting information.

Instead of guessing which source is correct, the discrepancy should be recorded for further verification.

---

# 3. Contact Intelligence Classification

Contact information should also be classified by source.

| Contact Type        | Meaning                                                    |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Public / Direct     | Published by the person or company through a public source |
| Company Route       | General company contact route                              |
| Third-Party Sourced | Found through an external data source                      |
| Unverified          | Contact exists in research but requires confirmation       |
| Verified            | Verification evidence supports the contact status          |

### Important

**Publicly available does not automatically mean verified or deliverable.**

Email discovery, email verification, and deliverability are separate research steps.

---

# 4. Confidence Framework

Research findings can be assigned a confidence level.

### High Confidence

Strongly supported by reliable and preferably independent evidence.

### Moderate Confidence

Supported by reasonable evidence but may require additional confirmation.

### Low Confidence

Limited evidence or unresolved uncertainty.

### Not Verified

There is not enough evidence to present the information as confirmed.

The purpose of confidence scoring is not to create artificial precision.

It is to make uncertainty visible.

---

# 5. Verification Checklist

Before a research finding is treated as ready for sales use, check:

### Company

* Does the company currently exist?
* Is the business currently active?
* Does the current operating model match the research objective?
* Has acquisition, closure, merger, or rebranding been checked where relevant?

### Account Fit

* Does the company match the ICP?
* Is there a documented reason for targeting it?
* Is the account rationale supported by evidence?

### Trigger

* Is the signal observable?
* Is it current enough to matter?
* Is it an actual signal rather than an assumption?
* Is the difference between signal and buying intent clearly stated?

### Decision-Maker

* Is the person currently associated with the company?
* Is the current role confirmed?
* Is the role relevant to the problem or buying process?
* Is the stakeholder classification a fact or an ABM hypothesis?

### Contact

* What is the source?
* Is it public/direct or third-party sourced?
* Has verification been performed?
* Is deliverability being claimed without evidence?

### Evidence

* Are important claims supported?
* Are conflicting sources documented?
* Is uncertainty clearly labelled?

# 6. The Research Record

A strong account record can follow this structure:

| Field             | Purpose                                   |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Account           | Identify the target company               |
| ICP Fit           | Explain relevance                         |
| Account Rationale | Explain why the account matters           |
| Trigger           | Identify observable signals               |
| Decision-Maker    | Identify relevant stakeholder             |
| Role              | Explain buying influence                  |
| Contact Source    | Show where contact information came from  |
| Evidence          | Support important findings                |
| Confidence        | Show research certainty                   |
| Verification Date | Show when the information was checked     |
| Sales Context     | Convert research into usable intelligence |

# 7. Evidence Trail

The goal is to make the research auditable.

A simplified evidence trail looks like:

```text
Claim
 ↓
Source
 ↓
Observation
 ↓
Interpretation
 ↓
Confidence
 ↓
Sales Action
```

For example:

```text
Observed Business Activity
        ↓
Official / Public Source
        ↓
Relevant Account Signal
        ↓
ABM Hypothesis
        ↓
Decision-Maker Mapping
        ↓
Personalization Context
```

This structure reduces the risk of turning weak information into strong-sounding claims.

# 8. Handling Conflicting Information

Conflicting information is normal in B2B research.

Examples include:

* Different executive titles
* Different company descriptions
* Outdated professional profiles
* Different employer associations
* Conflicting contact information
* Old versus current business activity

The correct response is not to silently choose the most convenient result.

Instead:

1. Identify the conflict.
2. Compare source quality.
3. Check recency.
4. Seek an independent source where appropriate.
5. Record the discrepancy.
6. Mark the finding accordingly.

---

# 9. Privacy & Public-Safe Research

MRMProLeads research is designed to avoid unnecessary exposure of private information.

Public-facing portfolio materials should not expose:

* Private client identities without authorization
* Private personal contact details
* Private phone numbers
* Confidential screenshots
* Internal research documents
* Sensitive account information

Public examples should use anonymization or demonstration labels when appropriate.

The purpose is to demonstrate the **research method and reasoning**, not disclose confidential information.

---

# 10. Research Quality Over Data Volume

More rows do not automatically mean better research.

A smaller set of well-researched accounts can be more useful when each record explains:

**Fit → Reason → Trigger → Person → Evidence → Sales Context**

This is the difference between:

### Contact Collection

and

### Account Intelligence

MRMProLeads focuses on the second.

---

# Final Evidence Standard

Before presenting a research finding as confirmed:

```text
Is the source appropriate?
        ↓
Is the information current?
        ↓
Can the claim be supported?
        ↓
Is it fact or inference?
        ↓
Is there conflicting information?
        ↓
Is the confidence level appropriate?
        ↓
Can sales actually use the finding?
```

If the answer is not clear, the information should remain:

**VERIFY / EVIDENCE CHECK**

rather than being presented with false certainty.

---

# The MRMProLeads Principle

> **Uncertainty should be visible, not hidden.**

The purpose of research is not to make every field look complete.

The purpose is to give sales teams information they can understand, evaluate, verify, and use.
