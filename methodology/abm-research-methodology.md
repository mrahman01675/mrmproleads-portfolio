# ABM Research Methodology

**MRMProLeads — B2B ABM Research & Sales Intelligence**

MRMProLeads uses a structured research process to turn target accounts into usable sales intelligence.

The objective is not simply to collect more contacts.

The objective is to understand:

**Why this account → Why now → Who matters → What evidence supports it → How sales can use it**

---

## The MRMProLeads Research Framework

```text
ICP
 ↓
Account Qualification
 ↓
Account Rationale
 ↓
Trigger / Buying Signal
 ↓
Decision-Maker & Buying Committee
 ↓
Evidence
 ↓
Sales Intelligence
```

Each layer answers a different question.

---

# 1. ICP Definition

Before researching accounts, the target profile needs to be clear.

Typical ICP dimensions can include:

* Industry
* Business model
* Company type
* Market
* Geography
* Technology environment
* Revenue or organizational characteristics where available
* Relevant business problem
* Target buyer or functional role

### Core Question

**What does a good-fit account look like?**

Without a defined ICP, account research can become a volume exercise instead of a qualification process.

---

# 2. Account Qualification

Once the ICP is defined, potential accounts are evaluated against the criteria.

The research looks at:

* Business relevance
* Product/service fit
* Market relevance
* Organizational context
* Technology or operational fit where relevant
* Available evidence

### Core Question

**Does this account actually deserve research and outreach attention?**

The goal is to prioritize relevance before contact discovery.

---

# 3. Account Rationale

An account should have a reason for being targeted.

The rationale may come from observable information such as:

* Business model
* Product direction
* Market activity
* Expansion
* Hiring
* Technology changes
* Partnerships
* Strategic initiatives
* Operational developments

Not every signal means buying intent.

The research therefore distinguishes between:

**Observed signal**

and

**ABM interpretation**

### Core Question

**Why this account?**

---

# 4. Trigger & Buying-Signal Research

A trigger is a reason an account may deserve additional attention.

Possible signal categories include:

| Signal Type | Example Research Question                                 |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Business    | What has changed in the business?                         |
| Growth      | Is the company expanding or entering a new market?        |
| Hiring      | Are relevant teams or functions growing?                  |
| Technology  | Is there a relevant technology environment or change?     |
| Product     | Has the company launched or changed an important product? |
| Market      | Is there a market event affecting the account?            |
| Operations  | Is there evidence of a relevant operational priority?     |

### Important

A signal is not automatically a buying signal.

MRMProLeads avoids converting weak evidence into false certainty.

---

# 5. Decision-Maker Mapping

Finding a contact is different from understanding who matters.

The research identifies relevant stakeholders based on the problem, product, or buying process.

Possible stakeholder categories include:

* Economic Buyer
* Executive Sponsor
* Functional Decision-Maker
* Technical Evaluator
* User Champion
* Procurement
* Operations
* Finance

### Core Question

**Who is likely to influence, approve, evaluate, or use the solution?**

The exact buying committee depends on the account and the solution being sold.

---

# 6. Contact Intelligence

Contact research comes after account and stakeholder research.

The objective is to connect:

**Person → Role → Account → Buying Context**

Where contact information is available, the source should be distinguished appropriately.

For example:

* Public/direct contact information
* Company-level contact route
* Third-party sourced contact
* Unverified contact information

### Important Rule

**Publicly listed does not automatically mean deliverability verified.**

Email verification and email discovery are separate processes.

---

# 7. Evidence Layer

Important findings should have supporting evidence.

MRMProLeads uses an evidence-aware approach that can distinguish between:

### Confirmed Information

Directly supported by an appropriate source.

### Research Hypothesis

A reasonable ABM interpretation based on available evidence.

### Unverified Information

Information that still requires confirmation.

### Data Discrepancy

Conflicting information that should be tracked rather than silently resolved by guessing.

---

# 8. Evidence Hierarchy

When researching an account, source quality matters.

A practical hierarchy is:

**Official Company Sources**

↓

**Government / Regulatory Sources**

↓

**Municipal / Public Records**

↓

**Professional / Industry Sources**

↓

**Third-Party Databases**

↓

**Inference**

The appropriate source depends on the research question.

High-value claims should receive stronger evidence whenever possible.

---

# 9. Research Confidence

Not every finding has the same confidence level.

A useful research workflow can classify information as:

**High Confidence**
Strongly supported by reliable evidence.

**Moderate Confidence**
Supported by reasonable evidence but requiring additional confirmation.

**Low Confidence / Unverified**
Insufficient evidence for confirmation.

This prevents false precision from entering the final sales intelligence.

# Research Quality Rules

MRMProLeads follows these principles:

1. **Do not guess missing information.**
2. **Do not present unverified information as confirmed.**
3. **Separate fact from inference.**
4. **Document important evidence.**
5. **Track conflicting information.**
6. **Do not claim email deliverability without appropriate verification.**
7. **Keep public, direct, and third-party contact sources distinct.**
8. **Protect confidential and private information.**
9. **Avoid unsupported company-size, buying-intent, revenue, or performance claims.**
10. **Optimize the research for sales usefulness, not database volume.**
